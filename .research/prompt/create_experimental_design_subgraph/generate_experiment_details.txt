# Experiment Strategy
────────────────────────────────────────
EXPERIMENT 1 – Dynamic Domain-Shift Evaluation ("Model decides when to re-calibrate")
────────────────────────────────────────
Goal:
• Show that the learnable α & T let the model react on-the-fly to unexpected mis-calibration, something fixed or hand-scheduled losses cannot do.

Setup:
1. Start with Mistral-7B on clean CNN/DailyMail summarisation (80 GB GPU fits easily).
2. After 30 k steps, *without stopping training*, inject a 30 % minibatch mixture of Reddit-TL;DR (noisy colloquial summaries) – a strong distribution shift that notoriously worsens calibration.
3. Compare four losses trained *continuously* through the shift: CE, TAHLB (best prior), SToPS, and SToPS-no-Soft-ECE (ablation).
4. Log α, T, Soft-ECE and ROUGE-L every 500 steps for another 15 k steps.

Expected result:
• Immediately after shift, Soft-ECE spikes for all methods.
• Only SToPS quickly decreases Soft-ECE (≈ –60 %) while α drops from ≈0.9→0.45 and T rises 1.0→1.6 within 2 k steps.
• CE and TAHLB stay over-confident (ECE > 15 %) or require manual re-schedule.
• ROUGE recovers to pre-shift level within 4 k steps (vs 8 k+ for pure Brier, 6 k for TAHLB) — proving no quality regression.
Demonstrates: SToPS can *self-detect* and *self-correct* calibration issues in non-stationary settings without human intervention, preserving convergence speed.

────────────────────────────────────────
EXPERIMENT 2 – Scaling Study Across Model Sizes & Memory Footprint ("One schedule fits all")
────────────────────────────────────────
Goal:
• Verify that the two extra scalars are sufficient for 7B→33B models and that the learned schedule generalises, removing brittle hand-tuning.

Setup:
1. Models: LLaMA-7B, 13B, 33B (fits in 2 × A100 80 GB with tensor-parallel; still well inside 2 TB RAM for dataloader caching).
2. Task: WMT14 En→De MT, 200 k training steps, identical optimiser & LR.
3. Losses: CE, TAHLB (best tuned per paper), SToPS.
4. Measure: BLEU, ECE, training wall-time, and *α/T trajectories*.

Expected result:
• BLEU: SToPS within ±0.1 of CE at every scale (and +0.4 over TAHLB on 33B).
• ECE: SToPS reduces mis-calibration by ~55 % (7B), 52 % (13B), 50 % (33B) versus CE; TAHLB plateaus at ~30 %.
• Wall-time difference ≤ +0.5 % vs CE (negligible), while TAHLB needs 3 trial-and-error runs to find a good schedule (+180 % total GPU-hours).
• Learned curves for α/T are *self-similar* across scales – no retuning necessary.
Demonstrates: The proposed self-tuning mechanism scales out-of-the-box, eliminating costly hyper-parameter searches for each new model size.

────────────────────────────────────────
EXPERIMENT 3 – Trustworthiness Impact: Hallucination & Adversarial Robustness ("Better calibration ⇒ safer answers")
────────────────────────────────────────
Goal:
• Highlight downstream benefits (hallucination, jailbreak resistance) that stem from better-calibrated probabilities, not just lower ECE.

Setup:
1. Fine-tune Alpaca-52 k on all three losses (CE, TAHLB, SToPS) under identical LoRA settings (fits in < 40 GB VRAM).
2. Evaluation:
   a. TruthfulQA-generation: measure factual hallucination (entity error rate) and yield-aware calibration (Brier).
   b. Flores-200 adversarial subset: perturb inputs with paraphrase + toxicity triggers; record performance drop.
   c. Automatic jailbreak detection: measure % of prompts where the model’s max token prob > 0.9 for policy-violating answer.
3. Inference uses the *temperature learned during training* for SToPS; baselines use T=1.

Expected result:
• TruthfulQA: SToPS entity error 18 % (CE 24 %, TAHLB 21 %).
• Flores robustness drop: SToPS −7 %, CE −12 %, TAHLB −9 %.
• Jailbreak confidence: SToPS high-confidence violations 3 % (CE 9 %, TAHLB 6 %).
• Generation quality (MT-Bench win-rate) remains statistically tied across methods.
Demonstrates: Calibration improvements translate into *practically safer* and more robust language models without sacrificing utility, validating the real-world value of SToPS beyond abstract metrics.

────────────────────────────────────────
Collectively, these three complementary experiments—online domain shift, scaling, and safety/robustness—show (1) SToPS’s ability to self-adapt, (2) its hyper-parameter-free portability, and (3) the concrete downstream gains that better calibration provides, firmly establishing the effectiveness of the proposed method.
